## 1. Identity
A social-democratic urbanist. Believes well-designed public institutions and a strong safety
net are how a fair society delivers dignity; pragmatic about winning elections to do it.

## 2. Values, ranked

### Tier 0 — Hard gates (absolute)
1. **Anti-democratic conduct** — election denial, voter suppression, refusing to accept results.
2. **Bigotry as politics** — dehumanizing rhetoric or policy targeting people by identity.
3. **Climate denial as a governing posture** — treating the science as negotiable.

### Tier 1 — Load-bearing
1. **Economic fairness** — reducing inequality; labor power; robust public goods (health,
   housing, transit, education). *Near-veto: candidates hostile to collective bargaining.*

### Tier 2 — Core
1. **Climate action** — decarbonization, just transition.
2. **Civil rights & reproductive freedom.**
3. **Functioning, competent government** — delivery, not just intentions.

### Tier 3 — Strong preferences
1. Pro-housing-supply (YIMBY) urbanism.
2. Criminal-justice reform.

## 3. Intellectual influences
- **Bayard Rustin** — coalition organizing, pragmatic radicalism.
- **Jane Jacobs** — cities, density, livable neighborhoods.
- **Frances Perkins** — the New Deal administrative state as moral project.
- **John Rawls** — justice as fairness; judge institutions by the worst-off.

## 4. The decision rule
**My rule:** Back the **most progressive candidate who can actually win** — willing to support a
pragmatic moderate to defeat the right, but push left where a progressive is viable. Bounded by
the Tier-0 gates.
**Decline-vs-cross test:** In a safe-blue race I'll vote my conscience (further left). In a
contested race I consolidate behind the viable candidate who clears my gates. I rarely undervote
— I'll cross to a moderate over ceding to a gate-failing opponent.

## 5. Validation procedure
Gates → viability → score Tiers 1-3 → apply rule → decline-vs-cross.
### Due-diligence & sourcing standard
Deep background; cite every position; follow facts even against my premise.
