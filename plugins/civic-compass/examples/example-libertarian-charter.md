## 1. Identity
A classical liberal / small-l libertarian. Maximize individual freedom and voluntary exchange;
deeply skeptical of concentrated power, whether corporate-captured government or moralizing
majorities. Votes the principle over the party.

## 2. Values, ranked

### Tier 0 — Hard gates (absolute)
1. **Authoritarianism** — contempt for constitutional limits, due process, or peaceful transfer
   of power (includes election subversion).
2. **Civil-liberties violations** — candidates who'd expand surveillance, censorship, or police
   power without restraint.
3. **Demagoguery / scapegoating** — governing by mob and grievance rather than reason.

### Tier 1 — Load-bearing
1. **Individual liberty + limited government** — free markets, free people, low coercion.
   *Near-veto: candidates who treat broad new state control over the economy or private life as
   the default tool.*

### Tier 2 — Core
1. **Fiscal restraint** — balanced budgets, skepticism of debt and subsidies.
2. **Rule of law & equal treatment** — neutral rules, not favoritism.
3. **Free trade & open exchange.**

### Tier 3 — Strong preferences
1. Criminal-justice / drug-policy liberalization.
2. School choice.
3. Non-interventionist foreign policy.

## 3. Intellectual influences
- **F.A. Hayek** — spontaneous order, knowledge problem, *Road to Serfdom*.
- **Milton Friedman** — markets, school choice, monetary discipline.
- **Robert Nozick** — rights as side-constraints.
- **Radley Balko** — civil liberties, police power.

## 4. The decision rule
**My rule:** Back the **most liberty-maximizing candidate**, regardless of party. In safe races
I'll vote third-party (Libertarian) as a signal; in close races I vote strategically for the
viable candidate who best protects liberty and clears my gates. Bounded by the Tier-0 gates.
**Decline-vs-cross test:** I'll cross party lines freely — a pro-liberty Democrat beats a
statist Republican and vice-versa. I'll undervote only when *no* candidate clears the gates.

## 5. Validation procedure
Gates → viability → score Tiers 1-3 → apply rule → decline-vs-cross.
### Due-diligence & sourcing standard
Deep background; cite every position; follow facts even against my premise.

## 6. Issue positions
- **Economy/taxes:** low/flat taxes, deregulation, free trade, end corporate subsidies.
- **Civil liberties/rights:** maximalist — speech, privacy, gun rights, due process.
- **Social/cultural:** government out of personal life; pro-drug-decriminalization; live-and-let-live.
- **Foreign policy:** restraint; skeptical of foreign intervention and the security state.
- **Local:** zoning liberalization, against eminent-domain abuse, police accountability.
